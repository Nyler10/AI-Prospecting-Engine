# Ideal Customer Profile & Qualification Rules

## Target profile

The system is tuned to identify organizations that fit one of these patterns:

- Organizations running **live events or sports coverage** that currently lack a strong connected-TV presence
- **Content-first organizations** (e.g., YouTube-first creators, media companies) sitting on a substantial content library with no dedicated streaming home
- Companies already operating their **own VOD platform** who could benefit from a more centralized or reliable delivery layer

## Qualification approach

- **Hard filtering over persuasive copy** — weak-fit prospects are filtered out at the qualification stage rather than relying on the outreach email to compensate for a poor fit
- **Additive framing, not rip-and-replace** — when a prospect is already using an existing tool (e.g., a competing platform or a general-purpose video host), the system positions the new platform as complementary and layered on top, not a wholesale replacement. This consistently lands better than a displacement pitch
- **Audience ownership as the sharpest hook** — for prospects that sell products or depend on fan/viewer relationships, the strongest angle is that third-party platforms capture the prospect's own viewer data. This is the single most effective wedge for vertically integrated or commerce-adjacent organizations, and the one most likely to get forwarded internally
- **Competitive positioning is pre-mapped per common competitor** — e.g., against a low-cost/self-serve competitor, the differentiators emphasized are reliability, support quality, and centralized management; against free platforms (like YouTube), the differentiators are revenue share and audience ownership

## Research requirement

No outreach is drafted without prospect-specific research first — distribution footprint, recent news, and business model are pulled before any email is written. Generic, non-personalized pitches are treated as a failure mode of the system, not an acceptable fallback.

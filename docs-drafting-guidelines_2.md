# Drafting Guidelines

## Core rule: draft only, never send

Every email the agent produces is saved as a draft. Nothing is sent automatically. A human reviews and approves every message before it goes out — this is a hard constraint on the output mechanism itself, not a policy the agent is trusted to remember on its own.

## Variant strategy

- Outreach is generated as **multiple variants with genuinely distinct strategic angles** — different openings or value propositions, not cosmetic rewording of the same email
- This lets the human rep pick the angle that fits their read of the prospect, rather than being locked into a single AI-generated take

## Tone by context

- **Detailed, first-touch pitches** — scannable, structured formatting works best: bold headers, a numbered "big ideas" structure rather than dense paragraphs
- **Warm follow-ups and lighter-touch channels (e.g., social/LinkedIn)** — shorter and more conversational tone performs better than a formal pitch structure

## Call-to-action rules

- **Specificity beats open-endedness** — proposing a specific day and time for a call consistently outperforms an open-ended "let me know when works"
- **CTA weight should match the relationship stage** — a cold first-touch email uses a low-pressure ask (e.g., a short demo request); early-stage or cold social outreach uses an even softer ask (a curiosity question, an invitation to share a reaction)
- **Warm-up messages serve a different purpose than pitch messages** — they exist to prime a conversation and set up specific angles for a call, not to close or convert on their own

## Accuracy constraint

Generated copy is constrained against fabricating or inflating claims — no invented statistics, business terms, or capabilities. If a claim can't be verified, it doesn't go in the draft. This constraint matters more than it might seem: an agent under pressure to sound persuasive will drift toward overstatement unless this is an explicit, checked rule rather than an implicit expectation.

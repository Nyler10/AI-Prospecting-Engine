# Drafting & Tone Guidelines

## Core rule: draft only, never send

Every email the system produces is saved as a draft. Nothing is sent automatically. A human reviews and approves every message before it goes out — this is a hard constraint, not a configurable setting.

## Variant strategy

- Outreach is generated as **multiple variants with genuinely distinct strategic angles** — different openings or value propositions, not cosmetic rewording of the same email
- This lets the human rep pick the angle that fits their read of the prospect, rather than being locked into a single AI-generated take

## Tone by context

- **Detailed, first-touch pitches** — scannable, structured formatting works best: bold headers, a numbered "big ideas" structure rather than dense paragraphs
- **Warm follow-ups and LinkedIn outreach** — shorter and more conversational tone performs better than a formal pitch structure

## Call-to-action rules

- **Specificity beats open-endedness** — proposing a specific day and time for a call consistently outperforms an open-ended "let me know when works"
- **CTA weight matches the relationship stage** — a cold first-touch email uses a low-pressure ask (e.g., a 15–30 minute demo request); early-stage or cold LinkedIn outreach uses an even softer ask (a curiosity question, "would love your thoughts")
- **Pre-call warm-up emails serve a different purpose than pitch emails** — they exist to prime the conversation and set up specific angles for the call, not to close or convert on their own

## Accuracy constraint

Generated copy is constrained against fabricating or inflating claims — no invented statistics, revenue splits, or product capabilities. If a claim can't be verified, it doesn't go in the draft.
